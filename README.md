# Customer-Churn-PwC: Churn Analysis Report

## Project Overview

### Objective
This project investigates why customers leave a telecom provider and where the business stands to lose the most revenue as a result. The aim is to move beyond a single churn rate and identify which customer segments, contract types, and service experiences are driving attrition, then translate those patterns into concrete retention priorities.

### Dataset
The analysis uses a customer-level dataset (02 Churn-Dataset.xlsx) covering 7,043 customers and 23 attributes, including demographics (gender, senior citizen status, dependents), account details (tenure, contract type, payment method, monthly and total charges), subscribed services (internet type, online security, tech support, streaming, and related add-ons), support activity (admin and technical ticket counts), and the churn outcome itself.

### Methodology
The work is carried out in churn_eda_v2.ipynb and follows a straightforward exploratory path. The TotalCharges field was cleaned by converting it to numeric and filling missing values for brand-new customers with zero tenure, and the churn outcome was encoded as a binary flag. From there, the notebook examines the overall churn distribution, breaks churn down across every categorical and numerical feature, and checks correlations among numerical variables. It then quantifies the revenue impact of churn, digs into why fiber optic customers churn more despite paying more, looks at how service bundling and support ticket volume relate to churn, profiles high-value churners specifically, and finally builds a simple additive risk score, using seven churn-associated flags, to identify currently active customers who most resemble past churners.

## Key Findings
The overall churn rate across the customer base is 26.5%. Churn is highest among customers with less than 12 months of tenure (48.3%), those on month-to-month contracts (42.7%), fiber optic internet subscribers (41.9%), customers without online security or tech support (around 42%), and senior citizens (41.7%). Churners are disproportionately valuable: they make up 26.5% of customers but 30.5% of monthly recurring revenue, with month-to-month contracts alone responsible for about $121,000 of the roughly $139,000 in monthly revenue lost to churn. Fiber optic customers present a paradox, paying $91.50 per month on average against $58.10 for DSL, yet churning more than twice as often; they also log nearly triple the support tickets, pointing to service experience rather than price as the underlying driver. Adding more bundled services is strongly protective, with churn falling from over 40% among customers with one or two services to under 6% among those with all seven, while support tickets show a sharp tipping point, with churn jumping from roughly 20 to 40% at up to five tickets to 70 to 83% at six or more. Within the top 20% of customers by monthly charge, 467 have already churned, accounting for a quarter of all churners and about $47,000 in lost monthly revenue, and this group is overwhelmingly month-to-month, entirely fiber optic, and largely without tech support. A simple risk score built from seven churn-associated flags correlates strongly with actual churn, rising from about 1% at a score of zero to roughly 74% at a score of six, and applying it to still-active customers flags 423 accounts, worth about $34,000 in monthly recurring revenue, as currently high risk.

## Strategies
Retention efforts should prioritize converting month-to-month customers onto longer-term contracts, since contract length is the single largest driver of both churn probability and revenue at risk, and could include incentives such as modest discounts or added services in exchange for a one- or two-year commitment. Service quality for fiber optic customers deserves a direct audit, since their elevated churn appears tied to support burden rather than price sensitivity; reducing technical issues and response times for this group could meaningfully close the gap with DSL. Support operations should treat repeated contact as an early warning sign rather than a routine issue, intervening proactively once a customer reaches their third ticket instead of waiting for escalation, given how sharply churn accelerates beyond that point. Encouraging adoption of security, support, and streaming add-ons can also serve as a retention lever in its own right, since more heavily bundled customers churn far less regardless of other factors. Finally, the 423 active customers currently flagged with a risk score of five or higher, representing roughly $34,000 in monthly recurring revenue, should be treated as an immediate, targeted outreach list, since they combine the same characteristics, month-to-month contracts, fiber service, limited support coverage, and short tenure, that most strongly predicted churn elsewhere in the dataset.

## Repository Contents
The repository includes churn_eda_v2.ipynb, the full exploratory analysis notebook with all cleaning steps, charts, and computed statistics referenced in this report, along with 02 Churn-Dataset.xlsx, the underlying customer-level dataset used for the analysis.

## Getting Started
Clone this repository, install the required dependencies (pandas, numpy, matplotlib, seaborn, and jupyter), then open churn_eda_v2.ipynb in Jupyter Notebook or JupyterLab and run the cells in order to reproduce the analysis and figures.
